Real Until Touchdown

The horizon paradox is adjudicated, with the state estimator out of the loop and the solver's own predictions dumped every solve and scored against what then happened. The QP's assembly is verified correct to 10−7. Its model stops being predictive at the next touchdown: at a quarter second of lookahead the attitude plan carries exactly zero information about reality. Every long-horizon configuration tried — the paper's own weights included — falls; the disagreement with the earlier report's "gait-consistent reference" prescription was right.

lookahead 0.10 s walks 70 s, no fall 0.15 s 12.6 s   0.25 s 6.8 s   0.50 s 3.6 s best rescue tried 25.3 s, still 3× short ground truth on both estimator stages throughout
The answer

The operative variable really is horizon × dt — because it is the lookahead T, and the stability cliff sits where T crosses the time to the next diagonal swap. Stance is duty × period = 0.25 s, so the time to the next touchdown is uniform on [0, 0.25 s], mean 0.125 s. At T = 0.10 s most solves never contain a swap and the swap is handled reactively by the maskChanged re-solve; that configuration walks until told to stop. The moment T reaches past the swap, survival collapses monotonically. The suspicion that the gait period was the relevant scale was nearly right: it is the stance duration, one diagonal's worth of it.

And the reason is the model, not the reference. The single-rigid-body model is dynamically consistent as implemented — a new unit test integrates the continuous dynamics independently through a mid-horizon contact change and lands on the QP's exact-exponential prediction to 10−7 — but it is not predictive: measured against ground truth, its attitude-rate prediction is ~35% wrong after one 10 ms step, its pitch prediction overtakes the reference error by 0.1 s, and at 0.25 s its roll prediction error equals the reference error exactly (0.200 vs 0.202 rad): past the swap the plan knows nothing. A horizon of 25 weighs one real interval against twenty-four of fiction, and u0 is optimized for the fiction. The measured signature is a limit cycle at gait frequency — force bursts saturating both stance legs at 400 N on each swap, the trunk pumped 25 mm above reference, roll growing until the topple.

01

The assembly is clean, and now provably so

An independent integrator agrees with the QP to 10−7 across a mid-horizon swap.

test_discretization_consistency poses a solve whose horizon contains a diagonal swap and whose moment arms differ at every step, then re-integrates the solver's own solution through an RK4 integrator written against the continuous model — not through the matrix exponential — and demands the condensed prediction X = Aqpx₀ + BqpU match step by step. It does, to 10−7, at dt 0.01 and 0.04. The contact/reference index offset, the per-step arms, the shared mean-yaw frame and the gravity column are all exercised. Whatever is wrong with a long horizon, it is not an implementation bug in the QP, and that class of explanation is now closed by a test rather than by inspection.

02

The model diverges from reality faster than the reference does

Every solve now dumps its own predicted trajectory; later records score it.

With ground truth in the loop, each dump record's current state is the true state, so the plan written at time t can be scored against the record nearest t + k·dt with no log parsing at all. Over the healthy 70 s baseline (horizon 10, dt 0.01):

Read the pitch row against the gait itself: the trunk's whole pitch excursion is ±22 mrad, and the model's prediction of it is 21 mrad wrong after 0.1 s — and already more wrong than the constant reference. Height is the exception: z prediction stays inside the reference error, which is why the height channel is not what breaks. The attitude-rate error is ~35% of the signal after a single step; that is not a discretization artifact, it is a forcing the model does not contain. The obvious candidate is measured in the mass budget: the legs are 8.3 of 15.2 kg — 55% of the robot swings at 2 Hz and the model calls it massless. MIT's machines put roughly a tenth of their mass in the legs, which is the licence behind the paper's 0.3–0.5 s references that this platform does not have.

03

The error breaks exactly at the touchdown

Keying every prediction to the swap shows where the model ends.

Drawn from run b1's data, normalized per component (figs/mpc/horizon_audit/b1_switch_error.png is the measured original). Within an unbroken stance the model is worth using; across the swap it is not. At horizon 25 the same analysis adds a second face of the same blind spot: plans launched from the first ~0.12 s of a stance — the contact settle window the estimator report measured — are bad over their whole length. The touchdown transient, the foot punching in and rebounding through compliant contact, is exactly what an instantaneous, impact-free contact model cannot carry.

Each row kills a hypothesis. Terminal cost (e3): ×25 on the last step's weights changes the fall time by under a second — the earlier report's step-03 hypothesis is dead. Support polygon (e4): duty 0.6 buys two seconds. Speed (e5): at 0.15 m/s it falls faster — the instability cannot even trot in place, so it owes nothing to stride demand or friction. The paper's full weight profile (e8): roll/pitch at 0.25 and yaw at 10 falls like the original — the lighter attitude weights surrender the roll authority that was containing the limit cycle. Only e2 moves: cutting the velocity and rate weights to the public Cheetah numbers (wz 0.3, v 0.2/0.2/0.1) while keeping roll/pitch at 1 stretches survival 4× — those channels carry the gait-frequency content the fiction is worst at, and weighting them less couples less fiction into u0. It is a real lever and it is still 3× short of the 0.10 s configuration, with a cost measured separately: at horizon 10 the same velocity weights track 0.23 m/s of a commanded 0.55, because this config's speed tracking lives entirely in the vx weight.

The short end, and what the estimator changes

Lookahead 0.05 s (e9) survives 92 s on ground truth — the earlier sweep's 4–5 s failures at dt 0.005 were estimator-in-loop. But it runs visibly rough (trunk 6 mm low, attitude jitter several times the baseline's): a short horizon is a high-gain controller, stable on clean state and amplifying estimator noise into a fall. The U-shape is therefore: twitchy at 0.05 s, clean at 0.10 s, fictional past 0.15 s. The shipped 0.10 s is not a compromise that better tuning will someday escape; it is the measured validity limit of this model on this robot.

05

The estimator-in-loop fall: a different verdict than proposed

Foot-z deterioration is a late symptom. The growing error is a frame, not a foot.

One instrumented run with the estimator back in the loop (0.55 m/s, horizon 10): trot from t = 18.6 s, visible degradation from ~38 s, collapse at 48.5 s — the familiar ~30 s lifetime against 70+ s on ground truth. Ordered by onset:

So the proposed chain — foot-z estimate deteriorates, foot believed below ground, phantom contact force — runs backwards: the foothold estimates stay clean until the gait is already failing, and the acceleration spikes date the collapse, not its cause. What is growing from the first stride is a frame error. The MPC's velocity reference is built in the _desYaw world; the measured velocity lives in the estimator's world, rotated by the accepted, unobservable yaw drift. The difference is a phantom lateral velocity vx·δψ on a channel weighted 1, and the controller obligingly leans on it. The integral seals it: over 28 s of trot the gap between estimated and true lateral displacement is −1.12 m, and −∫vxδψ dt predicts −1.01 m — 90%. The robot genuinely walked 0.49 m to the right of where it believes it went.

The fix this points at — proposed, not landed

Build the velocity reference in the heading frame: rotate the commanded twist by the measured yaw (_yawUnwrapped) instead of _desYaw where _solveMPC() and _integrateBodyReference() construct v, keeping _desYaw only as the yaw channel's own reference. Heading hold is untouched; the accepted yaw drift simply stops leaking into a weighted translational channel. This is not the previously declined yaw-bias calibration — the drift stays; it just stops steering.

06

What to do

Ordered by measured payoff.
settled

Keep horizon = 10, dt = 0.01, solve_period = 0.01, and stop treating the short horizon as a deficiency. Reactive handling of the swap plus a horizon inside the model's validity window is the correct operating point for a heavy-legged robot on this model class. A gait-consistent reference[k] would decorate a plan whose dynamics are fiction past the swap; the disagreement with that prescription is upheld by e3 and e8.

next change

The heading-frame velocity reference above. It is a few lines in MdlTrot, it addresses the one estimator error that grows, and with it the full-estimator lifetime has room to approach the ground-truth 70 s. Verify against lateral drift and lifetime, not against velocity RMS.

second lever

The −54 mm/s vertical velocity bias, unchanged since the estimator report: high_suspect_number is still the lever that moves bias and blind time the same direction. It is the remaining constant in-loop error once the frame leak is closed.

if lookahead is ever needed

It is model work, not tuning: the two measured gaps are the swing-leg reaction wrench (55% of mass) and the touchdown transient. A leg-momentum feedforward in the omega rows, or a contact model with the 0.12 s settle in it, would be the honest prerequisites. Weights cannot buy it — e2 is the best weights can do.

tooling kept

mpc.dump_path (per-solve prediction audit), mpc.terminal_weight_scale (default 1, today's cost), a reset() warning when solve_period > dt — the u0-held-too-long impulse error, confirmed as suspected — and test_discretization_consistency. All default-off or behavior-preserving; 12/12 tests pass.

Eleven headless runs, MuJoCo Go2, driven through a pty; ground-truth runs are noise-free in the loop, and the repeated baseline reproduced fall/no-fall, distance (35.2 vs 34.9 m) and height, though fine attitude RMS scatters with keypress timing (roll 8 vs 37 mrad between identical baselines) — gross outcomes are the trusted metric here. Audit records carry [t, x₀, Xref, U, Xpred, contact] per solve; analysis scripts and dumps live in the session scratchpad, measured figures in figs/mpc/horizon_audit/. Weight profiles labelled "Cheetah-Software" are the public MIT trot values. Note for the log directory: supervisor.log.file_name defaults to deneme.mat, and these runs overwrote it; the estimator-in-loop run logged to stage3.mat instead.
