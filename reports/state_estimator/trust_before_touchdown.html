quadcontrol · state estimator assessment

Trust Before Touchdown

The position/velocity filter is sound. What it is being told about contact is not: it grants full trust to feet that are still 15–30 mm in the air and falling at up to 1 m/s.

source bin/estimation_imu_attitude.mat, 30 s Go2 trot attitude imu passthrough vcmd 0.15 m/s gait 0.5 s, duty 0.5 confirmed on 2 fresh 26 s runs
Verdict

With attitude_source = "imu" the attitude error is 0.42 / 0.22 / 0.04 mrad RMS — exact, by construction. Every remaining error is the eighteen-state Kalman filter, and essentially all of it enters through one channel: the velocity measurement rows, whose error is exactly −vfootworld.

Averaged over the trot with the weights the filter actually applied, that channel injects a standing velocity bias of [+31, −20, +25] mm/s. Better than 90% of it is produced in the first quarter of scheduled stance, before the foot has reached the ground.

01

The velocity error is locked to contact trust

Not to time, not to the accelerometer, not to attitude.

Binning the velocity error by gait phase separates the cycle cleanly. When the trust ramp is closed the vertical error sits near −15 mm/s. When it opens to 1.00, the error deepens to −65 and −83 mm/s. The measurement the filter believes most is the one making it worst.

02

Because the feet are still in the air

The gait schedule and the ground disagree by 5–16% of stance.

A stance foot should sit at 21.8 mm and not move. Resolving the logged true foot positions over each leg's own scheduled stance shows that the second half of stance is exactly that — flat height, velocity under 1 cm/s in every axis, right up to liftoff. Liftoff is on schedule. Touchdown is not.

One scheduled stance interval, 250 ms. The trust ramp is anchored to the schedule, so it reaches 1.00 at phase 0.20 — after the foot has landed, but squarely inside the impact transient, which does not settle until roughly 0.35. Everything the filter learns in that band is wrong.

The correlation is the point. How late a foot lands is predicted almost perfectly by how high it was when the schedule declared stance — +0.95 to +0.97 across all four legs, over 44 events each. This is not contact noise; it is a deterministic geometric shortfall.

RR is the worst case and worth stating plainly: at scheduled stance phase 0.15, with the filter weighting it at 0.76, the RR foot is 28 mm above the ground travelling at (−0.19, +0.79, −1.00) m/s. That single leg contributes a +127 mm/s lateral term to the filter's velocity measurement, and it is where the run's y drift comes from.

03

Because the swing leg cannot follow its own trajectory

Commanded apex 49 mm; achieved 23 mm, a fifth of a cycle late.

The swing reference is a raised cosine that returns to the stance plane with zero height and zero vertical velocity at the end of swing. On paper the foot arrives on time and softly. Measured against the same reference, the leg reaches less than half the commanded height and peaks at u = 0.71 instead of 0.50 — the signature of a second-order lag driven well above its bandwidth.

The last column is the defect. At the instant swing ends the command is back at the ground and the foot is still 16–27 mm above it. That is precisely the 36–53 mm world height in step 02, and the rear legs' larger lag is why RL and RR land latest.

Compounding, at the same instant

At the schedule's swing→stance boundary MdlTrot::update() switches the leg from setCartesianForceCommand(…, _swing_kp, _swing_kd, 0) to (…, zero, zero, f_foot_B). The Cartesian gains go to zero while the foot still has 16–27 mm to descend: the leg stops tracking exactly when tracking still matters, and is handed an MPC ground reaction force it cannot produce because there is no ground. Applied at the foot that force points down, so it accelerates the leg into the floor — which is why impact velocities reach 1.0 m/s, above free fall from the same height.

04

What the trust profile is worth

Predicted offline from the logged truth, then confirmed in the sim.

The filter's velocity rows assert vbody = −Rbw(ω×p + J q̇), which is exact when the foot is world-stationary. Its error is therefore exactly −vfootworld. Re-weighting the logged true foot velocities under different trust profiles predicts the injected bias without touching the code.

All values mm/s. Widening the window cuts velocity RMS by 2.6× in x / y and 2.8× in z, exactly as the offline table predicted. And removing the ramp entirely brings the robot down inside two seconds of trotting — the ramp is load-bearing, it is simply the wrong shape.

The metric trap in that last column

Velocity got better in every axis and the position error got 6× worse. Both are true. The default configuration contains two independent x biases that happen to cancel: the touchdown transient pushes vx up by about +5 mm/s, while a genuinely planted foot creeps forward at +10 mm/s through late stance, which pushes the estimate down by the same amount. Remove the first and the second is exposed: the x bias flips sign from +4.8 to −4.2 mm/s.

This is the same “one error hiding another” pattern stateestimator.toml already documents for mahony_ki and for simnoise. It applies to trust_window too. Tune on velocity RMS; position drift will lie to you.

06

Where to spend the effort

Ordered by measured effect, not by ease.
root cause

Land the foot on time. The swing leg reaches half its commanded height and arrives a fifth of a cycle late; swing_kp_cartesian = 500 with swing_kd_cartesian = 8 is a badly underdamped loop driven at 4 Hz. Raising the gains, and not zeroing them at the schedule boundary until contact is actually made, fixes the estimator and the MPC's contact schedule at once.

Everything else on this list is a way of tolerating this defect rather than removing it.

do first

Split the trust ramp in two. The physics is asymmetric — liftoff is on schedule, touchdown is not — so a single symmetric window is the wrong shape. A separate ramp-in delay and width takes the injected bias from 36 to 11 mm/s, within 1 mm/s of a perfect-contact oracle. Small change, largest measured return.

reconsider

The comments in MdlPosVelEstimator.cc and stateestimator.toml argue that an open-loop gait's schedule “is not an estimate of where a foot is in stance, it is the definition,” and that a contact detector can therefore add nothing. That premise is now measurably false on this robot: schedule and ground differ by 5–16% of stance, deterministically and reproducibly. The MIT argument holds only when the swing controller lands on time. Either make that true, or the detector earns its place after all.

cheap

Set foot_ground_height to the measured 0.0203 m, and re-tune on velocity RMS rather than position drift for the reason in step 04.
