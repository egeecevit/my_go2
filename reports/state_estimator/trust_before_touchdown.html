Trust Before Touchdown

The filter granted full weight to a foot punching into the ground and springing back, saw only the rebound, and concluded the body was falling. Holding the measurement back until contact settled removed 43 mm/s of that error — and cost so much information elsewhere that the robot fell over. The change was reverted. The diagnosis behind it still stands.

status reverted — do not set this change posvelestimator.trust_settle_time = 0.12 bought vz bias −43.1 → +3.2 mm/s cost blind time 26.9% → 74.9% of the gait cycle
Outcome first: this was shipped, it made the robot fall, and it is reverted

Everything below about why the vertical bias exists is still good. The fix built on top of it was not. Setting the trust onset to 0.48 of stance while leaving trust_window at 0.2 collapsed the fully trusted plateau from 60% of stance to 12%, and the fraction of the gait cycle with no foot contributing a usable measurement went from 26.9% to 74.9%, longest blind gap 67 ms → 187 ms. With duty 0.5 there is no double support to cover that gap: it is dead reckoning on the accelerometer, four times per cycle.

The verification is the part worth keeping. I judged the change on velocity bias and RMS over two headless runs. Neither number can see a blind window — the filter's error stays small right up until the moment it has nothing to correct against — so a change that halved one metric and quadrupled an unmeasured one read as a clean win. A stability-relevant change needs a stability-relevant measurement.

And a fixed onset is the wrong shape regardless, which is the more durable finding. Contact settle time measures 0.121 s in the bad-landing regime but 0.000 s once the gait converges. A constant onset pays a permanent information cost for a transient problem — it is at its most expensive exactly when it is least needed.

What still stands

The velocity rows assert that a stance foot is a fixed point in the world. For the first ~120 ms after touchdown that is false in a way that does not average out. That is measured, and it is the real source of the vertical bias.

I also had the causality backwards in the first version of this report. I blamed the late, hard landings for the estimator error. It was the other way round: a filter reporting a phantom 45 mm/s descent had the MPC pushing up against a fall that was not happening, and the trunk bounced the feet off their own touchdown. Correcting the estimator took foot height at scheduled stance from 32–53 mm to 22 mm and impact speed from 1.5 m/s to 0.02 m/s with no gait change at all. That result is real; it is the reason the diagnosis survives the revert.

01

The trust ramp is not a ramp

It is a gate, and trust_window only decides when it opens.

A partly trusted foot has its measurement noise inflated by 1 + (1−trust)·high_suspect_number, and its measurement faded toward the current estimate. The weight it actually carries is the product, trust/(1+(1−trust)·100):

A foot at trust 0.90 contributes 8%, not 90%. So the ramp never blends anything — widening or narrowing trust_window just moves the instant the gate opens. That is the knob that matters, and it was pointing at the wrong moment.

02

The gate opened onto the rebound

A symmetric event, half of it rejected and half believed.

The foot does not stop when it lands. It punches about 9 mm into the compliant ground — height drops from 21.7 to 12.5 mm — and springs back out over the next tenth of a second. The sink happens while the gate is shut. The rebound happens while it is wide open. The filter therefore sees a foot that only ever rises, and a rising foot under a stationary-foot measurement model means a descending body.

Shaded band: where the old gate was fully open on a foot that was still moving. Weighting the simulator's true foot velocities by the real effective weight predicts −51.8 and −56.7 mm/s for the two baseline logs, against −42.0 and −45.5 observed. The mechanism accounts for the whole bias.

03

Why 0.12 s is measured, not tuned

It is a property of the ground — and of one regime only, which is the catch.

Read this as a measurement, not a recommendation. The number is honest: the contact really does take 0.12 s to settle in the bad-landing regime. It measures 0.000 s once the gait converges, which is what makes a constant onset the wrong instrument — see the outcome at the top.

Swing stiffness, swing damping and swing height all change how the foot arrives, and the settle time barely notices — because it is set by the contact, not the gait. go2.xml sets no solref, so MuJoCo's default applies, and it predicts the same window without anything being fitted. The parameter is therefore in seconds, not stance fractions, so it survives a change of period or duty.

A 187 ms gap is dead reckoning on a biased accelerometer for well over a third of a gait cycle, four times per second. It does not show up as bias because the filter is not wrong while it is blind — it is merely unconstrained, and the MPC is standing on whatever it says.

The x column got worse, and that is the honest result

Forward bias moved from +2.5 to −8.4 mm/s, and position drift grew with it. Two independent errors were cancelling: the touchdown transient pushed vx up, while a genuinely planted foot creeps forward at +9 to +10 mm/s through the whole of stance and pushes it down. Removing the first exposes the second.

That creep is now the largest remaining error and it is real, not a filter defect — a perfect-contact oracle bottoms out at the same −8.9 mm/s. Likely cause: the foot geom is a 22 mm sphere with rolling friction 0.01, and leg kinematics report the sphere's centre while the stationary point is the contact patch a radius below. A rolling sphere translates its centre while its contact point stays put. Worth checking before anything else.

05

What I built and then removed

The touchdown handoff. It did not earn its place.

I implemented the change we agreed: hold the swing Cartesian gains through early stance, released by the leg's own FK once the foot reaches the stance plane. It is reverted. Three reasons, in order of weight:

It measured as nothing. With the estimator fixed, the run with the handoff and the run with handoff_limit = 0 differ by under 1 mm/s on every axis — the feet now land before scheduled stance, so the latch clears on its first cycle and the code never runs.

Its premise was wrong. I assumed keeping the gains alive would land the foot softly. During early stance the position reference is the stance plane, which sits below an airborne foot — so tracking it drives the foot down harder, on top of the MPC force. In the run where feet still landed late, RR's impact went from −0.79 to −1.08 m/s. It made the thing it was meant to fix worse.

The gait could not have fixed this anyway. Sweeping the swing parameters, the best vertical bias reachable was −27 mm/s (swing height 25 mm), against +3 mm/s from the estimator parameter. The swing leg reaches ~33 mm of a commanded 49 mm apex and peaks a fifth of a cycle late; that is an impedance bandwidth limit, and no gain reaches past it without the feedforward term of paper equation (2).

Kept from that work: MdlTrot::getStanceDuration(), which the estimator needs, and the deletion of a stale TODO(ege) block describing code written directly beneath it.

06

Your yaw diagnosis, confirmed

Right about y. Not the reason x looked good.

Yaw error grows at 1.739 mrad/s against an injected yaw gyro bias of 1.69 mrad/s, and the bias estimate sits at exactly zero — w_corr is a cross product with the vertical, so yaw gets no excitation. The heading leak ∫(R(δψ)−I)v dt predicts +174.7 mm of the +182.8 mm of y error. 96%. Exactly as you said.

It does not fix x, though. The leak is second order in δψ and contributes only −8.8 mm there. What flattered x was the cancellation in step 04, which has nothing to do with heading.

The Mahony filter itself is healthy: roll and pitch error 2.5 and −2.6 mrad mean, 2.7 mrad RMS, with both bias estimates converging to within 0.6 mrad/s of the injected value. Nothing to fix.
